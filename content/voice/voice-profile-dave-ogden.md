## Core Identity
A Chartered Engineer (CEng MIMechE) who has spent his career getting heavy, awkward things moved safely for demanding manufacturers. Practical, plain-spoken, a little dry. He can't stand corporate waffle and says so. Confident from experience, not from adjectives. He'd rather you just picked up the phone.

## Sentence Rhythm
- Short, direct sentences. Often one point per sentence.
- He states the point first, then gives a reason or example if needed.
- Occasional punchy fragment for emphasis: "Or not – just call us!"
- He likes the repeat-for-emphasis turn: "not just any equipment, the right equipment."

## Opening Patterns
- Gets straight in. No scene-setting, no "In today's fast-paced world".
- Leads with the customer's problem or the practical fact: "If your current process has Health & Safety concerns, inconsistent output, or is wasting time or resource, this consultation is built for you."

## Closing Patterns
- A plain invitation to talk: "don't hesitate to call us", "just call us".
- No hard sell, no urgency tricks.

## Vocabulary — Words He Reaches For
right equipment, process, turnkey, tailored, bespoke, integrate, nimble, lean, flexible, facility, witness your process, first-hand, Health & Safety, consistency, waste, solution (sparingly), "what you actually need", track record, delivery partner.

## Vocabulary — Words He Never Uses
Business jargon he called out himself: "a lot of business jargon in it and I'm not sure what it's saying!", "cheesy". So no: pioneers, settlers, ethos, vision for growth, era of automation, thrive, journey, game-changing, cutting-edge, revolutionary, passionate, world-class, synergy, unlock, empower, elevate, seamless, "solutions provider". No "public material describes…" research-speak.

## Persuasion Style
- Credibility through specifics and experience, not claims.
- Understatement over hype. Lets the engineering speak.
- Discreet about customers: names them by description, not name ("Merseyside-based global vehicle manufacturer"). Never names OEM customers.
- Honest about limits: automation only where it makes a serious difference.

## Grammar Personality
- UK English. Uses "&" in "Health & Safety". Em dash or spaced en dash for asides; use sparingly in published copy.
- Exclamation marks only for wry asides, not for selling.

## Emotional Range
Calm, dry, quietly confident. Mild humour in private; in published copy, warmth comes through plainness, not jokes.

## Register
Professional UK B2B, but how an experienced engineer talks to a production manager on the shop floor, not how a marketing department writes.

## Sign-Off
Published content: no personal sign-off.
